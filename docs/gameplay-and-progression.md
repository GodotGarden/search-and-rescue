# Gameplay and Progression

## Core rule

This is an arcade-like co-op rescue game, not a medical simulation. Players are the rescue team; NPCs are usually casualties, hazards/creatures, or support characters such as dispatch and medical-center staff.

The repeatable interaction is:

```text
Read the scene → select useful equipment → use it → see a clear result → extract or resolve
```

The interesting decision is *what to bring and use*, not memorizing or performing a real clinical procedure. A first-aid kit, trauma kit, defibrillator, oxygen support, splint, exposure blanket, or similar item can appear as a later capability, but the game should never present its interaction as real-world medical instruction or certification.

## Rescue-station equipment dispenser

The station is the campaign's practical progression anchor. Before a callout, players can:

1. Read the dispatch summary and expected conditions.
2. Choose a small loadout from the equipment dispenser.
3. Travel to the selected region and respond.
4. Return to see incident results and an earned unlock.

Keep capacity limited. A player who takes rope may not also take extra flares; a player who packs a trauma kit may leave binoculars behind. Start with only one meaningful choice, then add depth after playtests show that players enjoy planning.

## Capability bands

| Band | Examples | Gameplay it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core responder | radio, basic response kit | Accept callouts and stabilize a basic casualty |
| Search and orientation | flashlight, map, compass, binoculars, glow sticks | Read terrain, follow clues, coordinate a search |
| Terrain and extraction | rope, backpack, stretcher, protective clothing | Reach difficult places and carry more safely |
| Hazard response | extinguisher, hose, fire-resistant equipment, flares | Control a scene before rescuing within it |
| Vehicle support | all-terrain ambulance, vehicle stretcher, extra supplies | Carry equipment and transport casualties farther |
| Specialist rescue | boat, scuba equipment, advanced fire response, helicopter | Open purpose-built later regions and incident types |

Each unlock must introduce a new choice, route, safety option, or rescue approach. Do not add an item merely because it sounds authentic.

## Incident design grammar

Build incidents from a few readable parts:

```text
region + casualty/hazard + evidence + condition + constraint + useful capability + extraction
```

Example: `coastal trail + injured hiker + dropped map + cold exposure + nightfall + flashlight/exposure kit + trailhead extraction`.

The same category can become harder by changing visibility, terrain, route length, required equipment, or coordination pressure—not simply by adding more NPCs or health bars.

## Co-op coordination

Two players can split up to search, regroup for extraction, carry different kits, and point out clues. Early co-op does not need voice chat because the intended players share a room. If remote co-op becomes a goal later, start with contextual pings and quick status calls such as “casualty found,” “need rope,” and “return to vehicle.”

## What stays out of scope initially

- Detailed medical procedures, gore, dosage calculations, IV operation, or treatment-order quizzes.
- A large unrestricted inventory or crafting system.
- Complex vehicle loadout management.
- Character classes or forced specialist roles.

The first hiker mission needs one default kit, one clear equipment interaction, and one extraction. Everything else earns its place through later playtests.
